# Privacy name-model bake-off (22 Nov 2026)

Track 13. Local only: no AI or provider calls, synthetic data only. Kristy approved it, and on licences she said: "Don't skip the ones due to licence".

## The decision rule, stated before the numbers

A challenger replaces the current model only if it meets all of these:

1. It catches at least **10 points more names** than the current model, and the gain is not noise (paired McNemar test, p < 0.05).
2. **Asian names** (Malay, Chinese, Indian together) are caught no worse than today.
3. It wrongly hides **at most 3 points more facts** (dates, amounts, merchants, organisations, places).
4. It runs at **≤ 200 ms median, ≤ 500 ms p95 per message, and ≤ 1.5 GB RAM** in the form it would ship, on 2 CPU threads (Remy's setting).

Licence never blocks her own install. It only decides whether the model can be bundled or has to be an optional download she turns on.

The thresholds were set after a 24-case smoke test and before the full run. Because they could have been tuned to the result, the table below shows which models pass.

## Recommendation

**Pick GLiNER multi PII v1 (urchade, Apache-2.0), but don't switch yet. First build and check a working ONNX version of it (one small task, below). Confidence: high on quality, low on cost until that check is done.**

- It catches **80.5% of names against 45.0% today** (+35.5 points, p < 0.001). The 95% intervals (78–83 vs 42–48) don't overlap.
- It catches 100% of Malay, Chinese and Indian names, against 78% today. That includes Chinese characters, where today's model catches 0 of 29.
- It wrongly hides 1.8% of facts against 0.9% today: +0.9 points, **within noise** (p = 0.06).
- It fails rule 4 on memory in the only form that works today, fp32 torch: 2.0 GB of RAM (limit 1.5 GB). Speed is fine at 196 ms median and 259 ms p95.
- Both quantised forms I tried are broken, not just slower:
  - onnx-community's int8 ONNX export gives every word the same score (about 0.17), so it hides nothing;
  - torch dynamic int8 also hides nothing.
  These are bad conversions, not the model's quality. The next step is to export ONNX with gliner's own script (fp32, then onnxruntime int8) and rerun this corpus. If quality stays within noise and RAM is ≤ 1.5 GB, switch.
- The only model that passes all four rules as tested is the **ai4privacy DeBERTa**. It catches 58.2% of names against the pick's 80.5% (the 95% intervals, 55–61 vs 78–83, don't overlap), catches only 67% of Malay names, and is non-commercial. It isn't worth switching to while the pick is this far ahead.

**What it still misses.** About half of names cut off at a field width ("EMILY BE"), and 6 in 10 names glued to other text ("PAYMENTTOAHMAD…"). In statement rows it misses 1 in 4. Every model is weak on these layouts. So Remy's printed-form matcher for her listed names, and the hard patterns, stay as they are: the model is an extra layer, not a replacement. An unlisted third party in a cut-off statement row is still the main leak risk.

**Runner-up: GLiNER multi v2.1** (same author, Apache-2.0). It is close behind: 78.7% vs 80.5%, a small but real difference (p = 0.04). Its wrongly-hidden rate of 1.2% is within noise of the leader's. It uses less memory (1.6 GB, still over the limit) and would need the same ONNX export step.

**Why not the others.**

- **NVIDIA GLiNER-PII** catches the most names (85.4%, +4.9 points over the pick, p < 0.001). But it wrongly hides 9.3% of facts (p < 0.001) and is about 4× slower.
- **knowledgator GLiNER-PII** catches as many names as the pick (80.9%, within noise), but it hides 70% of organisations and 65% of places.
- **The DeBERTa and Piiranha models** catch fewer names, or over-hide. Piiranha misses 95% of English names.
- **Presidio + spaCy on top of today's model** adds only 15 points and wrongly hides 7.4%. It misses all Chinese-character names.
- **Running the pick together with today's model** adds nothing (80.8% vs 80.5%, noise) and wrongly hides more (2.5%, p = 0.004).

## 2. Like-for-like speed (the fair comparison)

Each model is shown at the precision it was tested in and with the other treatment. The **like-for-like pairs** are:

- the current model at fp32 against the challengers as tested (all full precision);
- the current model as installed (int8) against the challengers at int8.

Quality is shown at each precision, so you can see what quantising costs.

**What it shows:**
- Like-for-like at full precision, the current model takes 31 ms against 196 ms for GLiNER multi PII: about 6× slower.
- Quantising the current model to int8 costs nothing (45.0% vs 44.9%, the same).
- Simple torch int8 **breaks every DeBERTa-family model** (all the GLiNERs, both DeBERTas and Piiranha drop to 0–25% names caught). XLM-R survives at 41.7% (−11.8). So for the challengers the int8 speed is real, but the quality is not usable. A proper ONNX export is the only realistic like-for-like int8 path. That is the open step in the recommendation. Speed is on 2 threads at below-normal priority, with the PC also busy with other work (60–95% CPU). Treat absolute times as ±20%; the ranking is stable.

## 5. Cost to switch, and what we'd lose

- **Code:** one class. `ops/local/pii_model.Detector` keeps its interface, `detect(text, wanted) -> spans`. Inside it, the BERT token tagger is replaced by GLiNER's span decoding over its ONNX file. Option A uses the `gliner` package's ONNX path, but that package pulls in torch (about 1.2 GB), which we don't want. Option B (recommended) is about 150 lines of numpy pre- and post-processing on the onnxruntime and tokenizers Remy already has. There is no new dependency kind, and the manifest, SHA-256 check and fail-closed behaviour are unchanged. The `PEOPLE` / `PLACES` switch maps to GLiNER labels ("person", "location"). privacy_benchmark.py and the gate corpus test are rerun.
- **Install size:** 106 MB today → about 1.1 GB at fp32, or about 0.35 GB if a working int8 export keeps quality. It is still a one-off pinned fetch, like today. RAM today is 0.23 GB; fp32 torch needs 2.0 GB, and ONNX is not yet measured with a working export.
- **Speed and RAM:** see section 2.
- **Risk:**
  - A model change needs one mutation run, because redaction changed.
  - GLiNER's input limit is 384 tokens, so long texts need windows, as today.
  - The model was trained on synthetic PII and tested here on synthetic text. A check on real-shaped (still synthetic) statement extracts from Ledger Flow's review layouts is the next step before turning it on.
  - Ships OFF by default behind the existing model install, per release rules.
- **What we'd lose:**
  - The current model's tiny footprint and about 17 ms speed.
  - Its slightly lower wrongly-hidden rate (0.9% vs 1.8%, within noise).
  - Its LOC tag, which today drives place hiding. GLiNER's "location" label would need its own check before it replaces that.

## Method and clean-up

- **Corpus:** a seeded generator. The categories are English, Malay, Chinese (a quarter in Chinese characters) and Indian names in everyday messages; ALL-CAPS statement names; names split across lines, cut off and glued; organisations; places; Ledger Flow-style statement rows (layouts from REMY-PRIVACY-REVIEW.md: DUITNOW, IBG, TRSF, GIRO, JOMPAY, POS); facts that must survive; and shops named after people. No real person, account or statement was used.
- **Labels:** each model's own labels for people.
  - GLiNER: "person" at a threshold of 0.5; knowledgator and NVIDIA used their documented labels at 0.3.
  - Taggers: their name labels, with simple aggregation.
  - Presidio: PERSON only.
- **Environment:** an isolated venv with hash-pinned requirements (`uv pip compile --generate-hashes`). Models were pinned by commit and their SHA-256 recorded. All of it ran offline after download, one model at a time.
- **Clean-up:** everything is deleted afterwards (including the broken int8 export), about 11 GB in remy-t13/.tools/bakeoff. Remy's installed model in remy-main was only read; its files still match the pinned SHA-256.
